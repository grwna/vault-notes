The previous collection of verbs contained all possible verb endings: 
- su/す 
- ku/く 
- gu/ぐ 
- mu/む 
- nu/ぬ
- bu/ぶ 
- ru/る 
- tsu/つ
- u/う 
These endings in the end define how the verb is conjugated. 

All regular verbs end in 'u'
All negative verbs end in 'nai'

>[!note]
>The last kana does not decide the type. 分かる is an u-verb, not a ru-verb.

**Quick Cheat-Sheet to Spot Them**
1. **Does the verb end in something OTHER than 「る」?** (e.g., 書く _ka**ku**_, 待つ _ma**tsu**_, 買**う** _ka**u**_)
    $\rightarrow$ **It is 100% an u-verb!**
2. **Does it end in 「る」?** Check the sound before 「る」:
    - **a-ru / o-ru / u-ru:** (e.g., 創る _tsuk**u**ru_, 割る _w**a**ru_) $\rightarrow$ **u-verb!**
    - **e-ru / i-ru:** (e.g., 食べる _tab**e**ru_, 見る _m**i**ru_) $\rightarrow$ **Usually a ru-verb!**
	
# Verb Conjugation
Verb conjugation rules are the most complex ones in Japanese. Resources:
- [japaneseverbconjugator](http://www.japaneseverbconjugator.com/)
- [verbix](https://www.verbix.com/languages/japanese.html)
- [wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_verb_conjugation#Example_conjugations)
There can be 30+ ways to conjugate one verb.

- plain / present tense: taberu
- negative plain / present tense: tabenai
- past tense: tabeta
- negative past tense: tabenakatta
All of these also exist for i-adjectives: oishii, oishikunai, oishikatta, oishikunakatta.  
And for na-adjectives by using: da, datta, janai, janakatta.
## Negative Verbs
Conjugation rules for negative verbs
- **For ru-verbs:** Drop the 「る」 and attach 「ない」  
    Example: 食べる + ない = 食べ_ない_
- **＊For u-verbs that end in 「う」:** Replace 「う」 with 「わ」 and attach 「ない」  
    Example: 買う + わ + ない = 買_わない_
- **For all other u-verbs:** Replace the u-vowel sound with the a-vowel equivalent and attach 「な>い」  
	Example: 待つ + た = 待_たない_
- **Exceptions:**
    1. する → しない
	2. くる → こない
	3. ＊ある → ない

>[!note]
>Why are there three rules if theres only 2 groups of verbs?
>U-verbs ending in う would have to be changed to あ, this would not feel nice.

# Tenses
## Past Tense
- The past form of a verb always somehow ends on "ta" or "da". 
- In Japanese, there's only one level of past tense.

**Past tense rule (Group 1)**

| **Verb Ending**                      | **Sound Shift**   | **Example Verb**  | **Past Tense Form**   |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------- | ----------------- | --------------------- |
| **〜む, 〜ぶ, 〜ぬ**                       | **〜んだ** (_nda_)   | 遊**ぶ** (_asobu_)  | 遊**んだ** (_asonda_)    |
| **〜う, 〜つ, 〜る**                       | **〜った** (_tta_)   | 買**う** (_kau_)    | 買**った** (_katta_)     |
| **〜く** (Except 行く $\rightarrow$ 行った) | **〜いた** (_ita_)   | 書**く** (_kaku_)   | 書**いた** (_kaita_)     |
| **〜ぐ**                               | **〜いだ** (_ida_)   | 泳**ぐ** (_oyogu_)  | 泳**いだ** (_oyoida_)    |
| **〜す**                               | **〜した** (_shita_) | 話**す** (_hanasu_) | 話**した** (_hanashita_) |

**Past tense rule (Group 2)**
Remove る, add た

# Verb Stem
Verb stem (often called the **Masu-stem** or **$i$-stem**) is the core base of a verb that remains after you strip away its sentence-ending suffix.
 
**How to Find the Verb Stem**
#### 1. Group 2 Verbs (_Ichidan / Ru-verbs_)
These are the easiest. Just **drop the final る (_ru_)**.
- **食べる** (_taberu_ - to eat) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **食べ** (_tabe-_)
- **見る** (_miru_ - to see) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **見** (_mi-_)
#### 2. Group 1 Verbs (_Godan / U-verbs_)
Change the final **$u$-sound** of the dictionary form to its corresponding **$i$-sound**.
- **行く** (_iku_ - to go) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **行き** (_iki-_)
- **飲む** (_nomu_ - to drink) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **飲み** (_nomi-_)
- **話す** (_hanasu_ - to speak) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **話し** (_hanashi-_)
#### 3. Irregular Verbs
- **する** (_suru_ - to do) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **し** (_shi-_)
- **来る** (_kuru_ - to come) $\rightarrow$ Stem: **来 / き** (_ki-_)
